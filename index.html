Cavern

      
You are a caveman with a torch and a stone-tipped spear, deep in a dark cave. Something pale and eyeless lives down here. It hunts by sound and by the glow of your torch: running is loud, walking is quieter, crouching and crawling are quietest. Stab it three times to kill it. It takes three hits to kill you. Big open caverns, tight squeezes, side tunnels and dead-end grottos; the minimap only shows where you have been. Low passages will not auto-crouch you: press Crouch or Crawl (C / Z) to get through. You stay inside the passages: walls, floors, ceilings, and junctions all contain you. This prototype builds a new cave from a seed. The same centerline-plus-widths format can later be filled from a real survey.

      
Controls

      
    
        
  • Desktop: click to look around, WASD or arrows to walk, Shift to run, left click or F to stab, V to switch third / first person, C to crouch, Z or Ctrl to crawl, Esc to release the mouse.
    • 
        
  • Phone: the left pad moves you (push a little to creep, to the edge to run); drag on the right side to look; the big Stab button thrusts the spear; Crouch / Crawl for low passages; the ☰ button opens the menu.
    • 
        
  • Carlsbad loads Carlsbad Cavern (New Mexico): the Natural Entrance route down the Main Corridor to the Underground Lunchroom and elevators (the exit), and the Big Room loop. The layout is traced approximately from public National Park Service maps. Named places show under the seed when you're near them.
    • 
        
  • The glowing point is the way out. Open New cave for another layout, or type a seed and press Load to replay one.
    • 
        
  • Sound on / off mutes everything (dripping water, stream, echo, music). Music on / off or M toggles only the creepy score, which grows denser the deeper you go and the more you move. Sound starts after the first tap or click.
    • 
      

      
Survey format

      
Caves are a list of stations and legs. Coordinates are metres, with x east, y north, and z up. Each station has LRUD widths (left, right, up, down) from the centerline, the same idea used by Survex and Compass. You can open a .json or simple .csv file with that shape.

      
{
  "name": "Example",
  "entranceId": "S0",
  "exitId": "S12",
  "stations": [
    { "id": "S0", "x": 0, "y": 0, "z": 0,
      "left": 1.2, "right": 1.1, "up": 1.6, "down": 0.6,
      "kind": "entrance" }
  ],
  "legs": [{ "from": "S0", "to": "S1" }]
}

      
CSV can list id,x,y,z,left,right,up,down,kind rows, then a from,to header and the legs. If legs are omitted, stations are connected in order.
